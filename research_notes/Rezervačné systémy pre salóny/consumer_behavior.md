# Consumer booking behavior and expectations for local service businesses (salons, barbers, spas, massage, beauty, wellness) – 2022-2026 evidence

> Research note on method and limits: The web-search budget for this session was exhausted mid-task and the network egress proxy blocked direct fetches of almost every primary page (GetApp, Phorest, Booksy, Zenoti, Square, Twilio, Eurostat, ČSÚ, Bitkom, BrightLocal, Baymard, PubMed/PMC, PLOS, professionalbeauty.co.uk, etc.). All figures below therefore come from search-result summaries and snippets of those pages, not from full-text verification. Figures are labelled with their type: **[vendor]** = published by a booking-software vendor about its own users or a survey it sponsored; **[aggregator]** = a statistics round-up blog that re-quotes others (lowest reliability); **[independent survey]** = research firm / trade body; **[academic]** = peer-reviewed. Sample sizes/methodology are given wherever the snippet included them; when missing, that is stated. Healthcare studies are explicitly labelled as proxies.

## Key question 1: What share of appointments is booked online vs. by phone, and what share happens outside opening hours? (Mobile vs desktop, channel preferences: Google / Instagram / website / app)

### Takeaway
Across US and EU sources, a clear majority of consumers now say they prefer to book service appointments online (GetApp: 67% online vs 22% phone), and vendors consistently report that roughly 40% of online salon bookings are made outside business hours. Hard data on the mobile-vs-desktop split and on booking-channel share (Google vs Instagram vs website vs app) for salons was not found in accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
**US / global**
- 67% of consumers prefer to book appointments online vs 22% who prefer to call; the online-preference share rose steadily from 56% in 2021 (GetApp consumer survey, US; sample size not shown in snippet) **[independent survey, vendor-adjacent – GetApp is a Gartner software marketplace]** — [GetApp research](https://www.getapp.com/resources/research-online-booking-importance-of-appointment-scheduling/) as summarised by [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/scheduling-statistics) and [SimplyBook.me](https://simplybook.me/en/blog/online-booking-statistics)
- 94% of respondents said they would be more likely to choose a new service provider that offers online booking; respondents wanted to book medical appointments online more than any other type of service — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/resources/research-online-booking-importance-of-appointment-scheduling/)
- 59% are frustrated with waiting on hold and inconvenient office hours when booking by phone — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/resources/research-online-booking-importance-of-appointment-scheduling/) via [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/scheduling-statistics)
- 33% of consumers say they have booked an appointment at midnight or later; 48% say a difficult or outdated booking experience would make them switch to a competitor; more than half of millennials and Gen Xers would switch providers entirely if online booking were not available (original survey source not identified in snippet – treat as aggregator-level) **[aggregator]** — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/online-booking-statistics); [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/advice/appointment-scheduling-statistics/)
- "Over 40% of salon bookings happen after business hours" **[vendor – Booksy, own platform data, no method given]** — [Booksy Biz blog](https://biz.booksy.com/en-us/blog/get-clients-book-appointments-online); the same ~40% "outside business hours (evenings, weekends, holidays)" figure is repeated by [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/online-booking-statistics) with the vendor claim of "$1,200+ monthly revenue otherwise lost" **[aggregator]**

- Supply-side proxy from an adjacent Czech sector (Stolio audit of 45 Czech restaurants, 2026): 64% accept reservations by phone only and 91% have no real online reservation on their own website, "while guests increasingly expect to book online" **[vendor audit, small n]** — [Stolio blog](https://stolio.cz/blog/pruzkum-jak-ceske-restaurace-prijimaji-rezervace-2026)
- Bookio (SK/CZ vendor) marketing claim: clients "especially praise online reservation and SMS reminders" and salons see visit rates increase by 30% **[vendor claim, no method]** — [Bookio SK](https://www.bookio.com/sk/segmenty/kadernictva)

### Inferences
- The "≈40% of online bookings outside opening hours" figure is a vendor number repeated across Booksy/Fresha ecosystems; it is plausible and consistent with GetApp's 33% "booked at midnight or later", but no independent audit was found.
- German Bitkom data show a very fast shift (36% → 64% in two years) toward online booking in healthcare; hairdressing is mentioned alongside doctors in Bitkom's phone-avoidance finding, suggesting Central European consumers' expectation of online booking is spilling over from healthcare to beauty.
- Czech consumers are among the EU's most active online shoppers, so low online-booking adoption among Czech service SMEs (e.g. 91% of surveyed restaurants without online booking) points to a supply gap rather than a demand gap.

### Gaps
- No accessible source gave the mobile vs desktop split of salon bookings (vendor pages that likely contain it were blocked).
- No source quantified the share of salon bookings originating from Google (Reserve with Google), Instagram/Facebook booking buttons, the salon's own website, or marketplace apps.
- Eurostat's ICT-usage indicator for "arranging an appointment via website/app" was not retrievable; only Eurostat accommodation-platform data surfaced, which is not relevant.
- No Czech- or Slovak-specific consumer survey on beauty-service booking (Reservio, Bookio, Booksy CZ) was found; only vendor marketing pages.
- Full Booksy/Kantar Poland percentages (phone vs app vs Instagram) could not be retrieved.

## Key question 2: Typical no-show / late-cancellation rates and what measurably reduces them (SMS reminders, two-way confirmation, deposits, card-on-file, cancellation fees, automated waitlist, reminder timing)

### Takeaway
Realistic salon no-show rates from platform data sit around 2–5% (with hotspots near 10%), plus roughly 8% cancellations; the widely quoted "30% no-show" figure is an unsourced aggregator claim that conflicts with vendor platform data. Online deposits (Phorest: −65%, 5.4% → 1.9%), cancellation fees/card-on-file (Square: −21% cancellations+no-shows), online booking itself (Phorest: −49% no-shows) and SMS reminders (healthcare RCT meta-analyses: attendance +11% pooled; no-shows down up to 38%) all have quantified effects.

### Cited Findings
**Baseline rates**
- Phorest platform data (UK & Ireland, post-reopening April 2021): 5% of clients failed to show for hair appointments without notice; in some regions almost 10% **[vendor platform data]** — [HJI reporting Phorest](https://hji.co.uk/client-no-shows-the-worst-areas-for-missed-appointments)
- Phorest: among salons using online deposits, no-shows fell 65%, from 5.4% to 1.9% **[vendor platform data]** — [Phorest](https://www.phorest.com/gb/blog/guarantee-client-commitment-online-deposits-feature/)
- Aggregate figure "salons have an 8% cancellation rate and 3% no-show rate; automated reminders and prepaid booking are the most effective tools" (source page blocked; appears to be Zenoti-sponsored survey data) **[vendor]** — [Zenoti, salon booking survey](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/salon-booking-survey-data)
- Contradiction: SchedulingKit claims "salons and spas have the highest no-show rate of any appointment-based business at ~30%, per Phorest 2023 Industry Report" **[aggregator]** — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/salon-industry-statistics); this is inconsistent with Phorest's own published 5–10% and 5.4% figures above and could not be verified; treat as unreliable. NoShowCost.com similarly markets a "30% rate problem" **[aggregator/vendor]** — [NoShowCost](https://noshowcost.com/salons)

**Effect of online booking and deposits**
- No-show rates are 49% lower for appointments scheduled online (Phorest) **[vendor]** — [Phorest via SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/salon-industry-statistics)
- A 25% deposit "cuts no-show rates by roughly half before a single dollar is forfeited"; 83% of consumers accept deposits for services over $100, with higher-value services facing less resistance; a majority want a clear refund policy displayed before paying a deposit **[aggregator – original surveys not identified]** — [SchedulingKit deposit statistics](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/appointment-deposit-statistics)
- Phorest client example: Togninis cut no-shows to under 2% with automated rebooking reminders **[vendor case study]** — [Phorest](https://www.phorest.com/blog/how-salon-software-increases-profit/)

**Cancellation fees / card on file (US)**
- Square (June 2024 press release, Square Appointments seller data): ~5% of beauty & personal-care sellers had cancellation fees enabled in 2021, nearly tripling by 2023; sellers with cancellation fees enabled saw a 21% decrease in cancellations and no-shows vs sellers without **[vendor platform data]** — [Square via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625191464/en); [Stock Titan summary](https://stocktitan.net/news/SQ/new-square-survey-finds-1-in-3-consumers-are-in-an-open-relationship-uvpv6fsd1ske.html)

**SMS / reminder efficacy (healthcare proxies, academic)**
- Systematic review & meta-analysis (10 RCTs, hospital attendance): reminders increased attendance by a pooled 11%; SMS reminders RR 1.14 vs telephone reminders RR 1.11 **[academic – healthcare proxy]** — [J Hosp Manag Health Policy](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html)
- Meta-analysis (JMIR, cited second-hand): SMS reminders reduced no-show rates by up to 38% vs no reminder; another meta-analysis: text reminders raised attendance likelihood by 50% in primary-care and outpatient clinics **[academic, via secondary summary]** — [YouGot.ai summary](https://www.yougot.ai/blog/reminders/appointment-reminders/do-appointment-reminder-texts-reduce-no-shows); [MailMyStatements](https://mailmystatements.com/does-texting-patients-reduce-appointment-no-shows/)
- Randomized trial on number/timing of reminders (US primary care & mental health): sending an additional reminder, vs the standard single reminder, reduced missed appointments among visits predicted to be high no-show risk **[academic – healthcare proxy]** — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/optimizing-number-and-timing-of-appointment-reminders-a-randomized-trial)
- Two RCTs (UK NHS, 2015 – **older**): stating the cost of the appointment to the health system in the SMS reminder reduced missed appointments vs a standard reminder **[academic – healthcare proxy]** — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137306)
- Pediatric clinic RCT: text reminders increased appointment adherence **[academic – healthcare proxy]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227159/); pragmatic randomized study of targeted text reminders reduced missed clinic visits — [Permanente Journal](https://www.thepermanentejournal.org/doi/10.7812/TPP/21.078)
- Vendor blogs claim automated reminders alone cut no-shows by 30–50% **[vendor, unverified]** — [Vocaly AI](https://vocalyai.com/blog/salon-spa-appointment-booking-reduce-no-shows)

**Automated waitlists**
- Vendor claims: salons running an automated waitlist text to a "pre-warmed" list fill 60–75% of same-day cancellations within an hour; other vendors report 60–70% recovery of last-minute cancellations **[vendor claims, no methodology]** — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-tools-hair-salons-fill-empty-appointments/); [BookingBee](https://bookingbee.ai/fill-your-salons-calendar/); Zenoti markets automated waitlist filling without front-desk intervention — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/platform/waitlist-management)

### Inferences
- Realistic planning assumptions for a salon booking system: ~3–5% no-shows and ~8% cancellations at baseline; deposits/card-on-file can push no-shows below 2%; the "30%" figure should not be used.
- Healthcare evidence supports SMS as at least as effective as phone reminders and cheaper; a second reminder adds measurable benefit mainly for high-risk clients (new clients, history of no-shows).
- Square's 21% figure is a comparison between sellers with and without fees, not an RCT; self-selection likely inflates it.

### Gaps
- No salon-specific study on reminder timing (24h vs 48h) or on two-way "reply C to confirm" confirmation rates was found; only healthcare proxies.
- No independent measurement of automated-waitlist fill rates; all figures are vendor marketing.
- No European (CZ/SK/DE/PL) no-show rate data for salons was accessible beyond Phorest UK/IE.

## Key question 3: What frustrates consumers about booking (abandonment, forced registration, unclear pricing, no real-time availability)?

### Takeaway
Phone friction (hold times, office hours) and outdated booking experiences are the top stated frustrations (GetApp 59%; 48% would switch providers). Forced account creation is a well-documented abandonment driver in e-commerce (Baymard: 24–26% of US shoppers abandoned a purchase in the last quarter for this reason alone); no salon-specific study on forced registration, unclear pricing or lack of real-time availability was found.

### Cited Findings
- 59% frustrated with waiting on hold and inconvenient office hours when scheduling by phone — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/resources/research-online-booking-importance-of-appointment-scheduling/) via [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/scheduling-statistics)
- 48% say a difficult or outdated booking experience would make them switch to a competitor **[aggregator]** — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/online-booking-statistics)
- Baymard Institute (US, n=4,384 adult online shoppers, 2022): 24% abandoned one or more carts in the past quarter solely because the site wanted them to create an account **[independent UX research, e-commerce proxy]** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/make-guest-checkout-prominent); later Baymard-based summaries put forced account creation at ~26% of preventable abandonments — [Growthegy on Baymard](https://www.growthegy.com/2026/05/26/cart-abandonment-science-why-customers-leave-checkout/)
- Secondary claims: guest checkout raises completion 14–35% vs forced account creation; sites switching to guest checkout see 15–30% conversion lift **[secondary/vendor blogs]** — [Krepling Pay](https://pay.krepling.com/guest-checkout-vs-account-which-strategy-converts-more-customers-in-2026/); [Mantas Auk](https://mantasauk.com/articles/guest-checkout-optimization-account-creation-costs-sales/)
- Hotel booking-engine best practice: mandatory login is "one of the most common reasons a motivated guest quits at the form"; recommended pattern is guest checkout by default with optional account creation after confirmation **[vendor guidance, hospitality proxy]** — [RateGain](https://rategain.com/blog/how-to-reduce-booking-engine-abandonment-2026-best-practices/)
- Bitkom: about a third of Germans avoid phone calls and prefer e-mail/online systems for appointments (doctor, hairdresser) **[independent survey]** — [Retail-News on Bitkom](https://retail-news.de/telefonieren-als-belastung-bitkom-studie/)

### Inferences
- For a salon booking flow, the e-commerce evidence argues strongly for guest booking (name + phone/e-mail) with optional account creation afterwards; a mandatory app download or registration step likely loses a quarter or more of first-time bookers.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying booking abandonment specifically caused by hidden prices or lack of real-time availability in beauty/wellness was found.
- No salon-specific booking-funnel abandonment benchmarks were accessible.

## Key question 4: How important are reviews, photos, displayed prices and "Reserve with Google" / Instagram booking buttons in choosing a provider?

### Takeaway
Reviews remain central (BrightLocal 2025: 71% of US consumers read local reviews regularly; Google is the review platform for 83%), though consumers grew more lenient on review count/recency in 2025. Price and past experience top the salon-choice criteria in Poland (Booksy/Kantar). Reserve with Google evidence is mostly hospitality case studies (89% booking conversion in a 16-venue trial); social-discovery statistics for salons come only from low-reliability marketing aggregators.

### Cited Findings
- BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 (US, n=1,026 adults, SurveyMonkey panel): 71% read online reviews regularly when browsing local businesses; Google is the most used review platform (83%); consumers check at least two review sites; share saying star rating does not influence them doubled from 5% to 9%; consumers focus on reviews posted within the last month; consumers use AI review summaries and, unknowingly, preferred an AI-written business response **[independent survey]** — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/); [Local Search Forum discussion](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/brightlocals-2025-consumer-review-study.62172/)
- Booksy/Kantar (Poland): top salon-choice factors are attractive prices, positive experience with the venue, and quality of services **[vendor-sponsored]** — [Booksy press office](https://biuroprasowe.booksy.com/jak-polacy-rezerwuja-wizyty-w-ulubionych-salonach-branzy-beauty-wyniki-badania-konsumenckiego-kantar-na-zlecenie-booksy/)
- Reserve with Google – Access Group / Collins (UK hospitality, 3-month trial, 16 venues): 89% booking conversion rate, +4.2% new-customer rate, £1.21m incremental annual revenue; Access estimates venues using RwG see ~2% incremental covers via ~4% uplift in both conversion and new-customer acquisition **[vendor case study, restaurants not salons]** — [The Access Group](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/blog/hos-reserve-with-google-integrations/)
- Reserve with Google example uplift: new users from Google Maps +35%, daily purchases +38% (business type unclear) **[vendor]** — [TIMIFY](https://www.timify.com/en/blog/how-local-businesses-use-reserve-with-google-to-increase-revenue-2/)
- Salon-software vendors position RwG as adding a "Book now" button in Search/Maps for "hair salons near me" searches (qualitative) **[vendor]** — [Simple Salon](https://www.simplesalon.com/reserve-with-google-answering-your-top-questions-about-boosting-salon-bookings/); [MioSalon](https://blog.miosalon.com/boost-salon-bookings-with-google-reserve-partner/)
- Social discovery claims (no primary source identifiable): "78% of salon clients discover new salons through Instagram or Facebook"; "80% of beauty consumers check Instagram before booking"; "68% trust a salon more with an active social presence"; "brands adopting social booking tools see a 14% lift in bookings" **[aggregator – low reliability]** — [Marketing LTB](https://marketingltb.com/blog/statistics/salon-marketing-statistics/); [Rudys.ai](https://rudys.ai/beauty-salon-statistics/); [ZipDo](https://zipdo.co/marketing-in-the-salon-industry-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Because Google is both the dominant review surface (83%) and offers a native booking button, a salon's Google Business Profile with live availability is the highest-leverage discovery-to-booking path indicated by the evidence; Instagram figures are directionally plausible but unverified.

### Gaps
- No quantified study on the effect of salon photos (portfolio, before/after) or of displayed prices on conversion was found.
- No salon-specific Reserve with Google case study with numbers was accessible (Fresha, Booksy and Vagaro pages blocked).
- BrightLocal breakdown by business category (beauty/health) not retrievable.

## Key question 5: Messaging expectations – SMS vs WhatsApp vs e-mail vs push; open rates; cost per message (CZ/SK/EU/US)

### Takeaway
Consumers treat appointment reminders as "urgent" and prefer text over e-mail by more than 2:1 (Twilio); Square's beauty consumers prefer text reminders (62%) over e-mail (31%) and phone (23%). WhatsApp and SMS both show ~90%+ open rates versus ~20–28% for e-mail (vendor benchmarks). Per-message SMS cost data was found only for Slovakia (Twilio from $0.0883); Czech, broader EU and US rates could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Square Future of Beauty (Wakefield Research, US/CA/UK/AU): for appointment reminders 62% prefer text messages, 31% want an e-mail confirmation, 23% still want a phone call; booking preferences were similar across countries but reminder preferences differed **[vendor-sponsored survey]** — [Salon Today on Square](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/square-offers-insight-into-consumer-beauty-trends); [Modern Salon](https://www.modernsalon.com/1089784/square-offers-insight-into-consumer-beauty-trends)
- Twilio Consumer Preferences Report (n=3,900 global consumers): the majority consider appointment reminders "urgent" messages; for urgent business communications texts are more than twice as popular as e-mail; LATAM consumers cite WhatsApp as a top-3 channel for nearly every use case **[vendor survey]** — [Twilio Communications Blueprint](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/report/the-communications-blueprint/overview/chapter-1)
- Twilio 2024 Global Messaging Engagement Report (n=4,800+ consumers in Brazil, France, Germany, Japan, UK, US): consumers pay little attention to brands' social media or mobile apps and prefer e-mail or text **[vendor survey]** — [Twilio press release](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/press/releases/twilio-study-shows-consumers-arent-paying-attention-to-brands-social-media-or-mobile-apps-they-prefer-email-or-text-instead); [Report](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/resource-center/2024-global-messaging-engagement-report)
- Salesforce State of the Connected Customer (15,000+ consumers and business buyers): consumers increasingly prefer digital, especially messaging, channels **[vendor survey, generic]** — [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/resources/research-reports/state-of-the-connected-customer/)
- Open-rate benchmarks (vendor blogs, methodology unstated; ranges conflict): WhatsApp 91–98%; SMS quoted anywhere from 32–40% up to 98%; e-mail 20–28%; WhatsApp CTR 45–60% vs e-mail 2–5%; SMS average response rate 45% **[vendor/aggregator – low reliability]** — [NimbleBiz](https://nimblebiz.ai/blog/whatsapp-open-rate-vs-email-vs-sms-benchmarks); [AiSensy](https://m.aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-statistics-for-businesses/); [Notifyre](https://notifyre.com/us/blog/sms-marketing-statistics); [Chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/whats-app-vs-email)
- SMS pricing: Twilio outbound SMS to Slovakia starts at $0.0883 per message **[vendor price list, as summarised]** — [Sent.dm Slovakia guide](https://www.sent.dm/en/resources/sms-pricing/slovakia-sms-pricing); [Twilio SK pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/sk); Czech price page exists but rate was not captured — [Twilio CZ pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/cz)

- For CZ/SK, SMS at roughly $0.08–0.09 per message (Slovakia) is materially more expensive than typical US SMS, which makes WhatsApp Business or push/e-mail attractive cost-wise, but no CZ/SK consumer-preference data on WhatsApp for appointment communication was found, so preference should be treated as unknown.

## Key question 6: Which booking-app features do consumers value most (specific staff member, waitlist, prepayment, loyalty, rebooking, account vs guest)?

### Takeaway
Direct consumer-ranked feature surveys for salon apps were not accessible; the indirect evidence points to 24/7 online booking, easy rescheduling/cancellation, text reminders and digital payment as the most valued elements, with industry rebooking rates averaging 45–55% (top salons 80%+). Data on demand for "book with a specific staff member", waitlist usage and loyalty-program impact was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Consumers most welcome automation for reminders (35%) and booking (34%) (Square/Wakefield) **[vendor-sponsored]** — [Salon Today on Square](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/square-offers-insight-into-consumer-beauty-trends)
- Square 2024 survey headline: "1 in 3 consumers are in an open relationship" with beauty providers (i.e. not loyal to a single provider) **[vendor-sponsored, detail not retrievable]** — [Stock Titan on Square](https://stocktitan.net/news/SQ/new-square-survey-finds-1-in-3-consumers-are-in-an-open-relationship-uvpv6fsd1ske.html)

### Inferences
- Since ~1 in 3 beauty consumers is not loyal to one provider (Square) and average rebooking is only 45–55%, features that drive rebooking at checkout and automated "time to rebook" reminders have the largest evidenced upside.
- The Polish result that two-thirds would adopt digital payment if offered suggests Central European clients are open to prepayment/deposit flows when they are presented as convenience (BLIK/card) rather than penalty.

### Gaps

- No quantified loyalty-program effect on salon retention from a 2022-2026 source.
- No data on consumer tolerance of prepayment (full) vs deposit vs card-on-file in the EU beyond the Polish digital-payment willingness figure.
