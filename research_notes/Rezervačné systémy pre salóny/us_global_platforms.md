**Method / source-quality note for the report writer.** Research was conducted 27–28 Sept 2026. Every WebFetch attempt (official pricing pages, help centers, TechCrunch, Fortune, Crunchbase News, Capterra, Wikipedia, etc.) was blocked by the network egress proxy, and the session-wide WebSearch budget ran out after the third batch. All findings below therefore come from **search-result snippets/summaries**, not from full pages. Consequences: (a) numbers should be treated as "as reported by the cited page" and verified before publication; (b) attribution of a given figure to a specific URL is to the search-result page most likely containing it; (c) several company-fact items I know from background (HQ cities, founders, founding years) could NOT be sourced in this session and are listed in the Gaps sections as "unverified background", not as findings. Prices are USD unless marked; "2026 price guides" from third-party comparison blogs (Pabau, SchedulingKit, Koalendar, GlossGenius blog, DoTheBeauty, Slotcut, Bizzly, CostBench) are competitor- or SEO-authored and may lag official pages by months.

## Key Question 1 — Who are the top players by scale / market share, and how have they grown 2023–2026?

### Takeaway
Scale leaders in the SMB beauty/wellness segment are Fresha (~130k businesses, ~35M appointments/month, $1.4B monthly GMV; unicorn since 2026), Vagaro ("hundreds of thousands of professionals"), Booksy (reported "346,000 users in 50+ countries", ~1,200 staff), GlossGenius/Genius AI (125k+ customers, ~$200M ARR, unicorn July 2026), Square Appointments (Square claims 600k monthly-active beauty & wellness sellers), Mindbody/Booker (enterprise-leaning; now inside the $7.5B Playlist–EGYM group) and Zenoti (30k customers in 50 countries, ~$174M ARR 2024, enterprise). In Europe, Treatwell (75k+ salons on marketplace) and Planity (25% of French salons, #2 in DE/BE) lead. No authoritative independent market-share dataset was found; the only "share" figure (top-5 = ~76% of online-booking traffic) comes from a low-authority vendor blog.

- 2024–2026 saw two new unicorns in the SMB segment (Fresha $1B, GlossGenius/Genius AI $1.15B) plus a near-unicorn (Boulevard ~$800M), signalling investor conviction that payments + AI monetization on top of booking is durable.

## Key Question 2 — Exact pricing and monetization models (subscription vs. "free + commission", processing rates, marketplace fees, SMS)

### Takeaway
Three monetization archetypes coexist: (1) **pure subscription** (Mangomint, Boulevard, Acuity, Phorest, Timely, Schedulicity, SimplyBook.me, Setmore, Zenoti, Meevo, DaySmart, Shore, Noona); (2) **subscription + integrated payments + marketplace new-client commission** (Fresha 20%/min $6; Booksy Boost 30%/min $10/max $100; StyleSeat 30%/cap $50; Treatwell 35%; Mindbody marketplace ~23.5% effective); and (3) **free software + payments margin** (Square Appointments free tier at 2.6%+15¢). The most consequential 2025–2026 change is Fresha ending its free-forever tier (sources conflict on timing: "early/March 2025" vs. "fully paid for everyone incl. legacy accounts July 2026") and Mangomint's Aug-2026 repricing to $120/location + $10/user.
